Thirteen filters stand between a satellite pass and a usable day/night pair

The ECOSTRESS census turned 218,385 CMR granule hits into 3,101 quality pairs across 158 stations. This page follows every single overpass at four contrasting stations through each filter, so you can see which one killed it — and why the largest loss of all is the one the census never writes down.

Where each lane loses granules

The chain forks at stage 5 and rejoins at stage 12. pair_station is handed the phase‑filtered set and never reads passed_qc — quality is attached afterwards, at census_ecostress.py:649, as a label on an already‑formed pair. So the QC‑passed count and the pair count sit on different branches, and subtracting one from the other means nothing.

Why so few pairs

A pair needs a day pass and a night pass over the same station in one nocturnal period. The ISS overpass local time precesses through all 24 hours in roughly 60 days, so most solar dates catch a station in only one half of that cycle.

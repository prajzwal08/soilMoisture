Evaluation Split Framework: OOS / OOT / OOST
==============================================
Date: 2026-05-18


SCIENTIFIC SUMMARY
------------------
Rigorous evaluation of a spatiotemporal model requires testing distinct types of
generalisation separately. A model that performs well only on stations near those
it was trained on, or only on time periods it has seen before, will fail in
real-world deployment. We designed a Stratified Environmental Split (SES) framework
with three independent evaluation axes:

  OOS  (Out-of-Sample):  Can the model predict soil moisture at locations it has
       never observed during training? Tests spatial generalisation — the ability
       to extrapolate to new fields, new landscapes, new soil types.

  OOT  (Out-of-Time):    Can the model predict during 2023–2025, a period withheld
       from training across all stations? Tests temporal generalisation and directly
       mirrors the primary deployment scenario: near-real-time daily SM mapping.

  OOST (Out-of-Sample + Out-of-Time):  The hardest test — held-out stations
       evaluated on the 2023–2025 period simultaneously. Success here demonstrates
       that the model has learned physically meaningful representations rather than
       memorising location-specific or era-specific patterns.

  JOINT_EVAL:  Fifteen flux+SM sites within OOS evaluate simultaneous prediction
       of soil moisture and latent heat flux (LE), testing the jointly trained
       encoder at unseen locations where both labels are available.

  FLUX_ONLY_EVAL:  Seventeen flux-only OOS sites (LE but no SM) evaluate LE
       prediction at locations where the model received no SM co-supervision
       during training. Comparing these results to JOINT_EVAL directly quantifies
       the benefit of joint SM+LE training for LE generalisation.


TRAINING STRATEGY
-----------------
The dataset contains a large soil-moisture pool (894 ISMN + flux-only sites) and a
small flux+SM pool (34 sites in training after 15 are reserved for joint-eval).
Training jointly from scratch risks the sparse flux signal (~34 sites) being
overwhelmed by the dense SM signal (~860 stations). A two-phase curriculum is used:

  Phase 1 — SM pre-training
    Input:    all 1,048 stations (717 train + 97 val)
    Loss:     soil moisture only (Huber loss on SM head)
    Updates:  ERA5-Land MLP, SoilGrids MLP, temporal transformer, SM head
              TerraMind encoder remains frozen throughout
    Result:   encoder develops rich spatiotemporal SM representations from a
              large, geographically and climatically diverse dataset before the
              sparse flux signal is introduced

  Phase 2 — Joint fine-tuning
    Input:    all 1,048 stations for SM loss
              + 34 flux+SM training sites for LE loss (SM + LE labels)
              + 88 flux-only training sites for LE loss (LE label only, SM masked)
              → 122 sites total contributing to the LE head
    Loss:     SM (Huber) + LE (Huber), weighted equally; missing labels masked
    Learning rate: lower than Phase 1 (e.g. 10× reduction)
    Result:   flux head learns LE from encoder features already optimised for SM;
              88 flux-only sites significantly augment the sparse flux+SM signal;
              SM performance maintained via continued SM loss on all stations

  Rationale: analogous to large-scale pre-training followed by task-specific
  fine-tuning. Phase 1 prevents the encoder from being under-constrained by the
  sparse flux signal. A "flux-only" intermediate phase is not used — it would
  cause catastrophic forgetting of SM representations learned in Phase 1.
  Flux-only sites (LE label, no SM) contribute only in Phase 2, expanding the
  effective LE training set from 34 to 122 sites.


SCIENTIFIC PARAGRAPH (for publication)
---------------------------------------
We assembled in-situ records from three independent observation networks: the
International Soil Moisture Network (ISMN), the Integrated Carbon Observation
System (ICOS), and AmeriFlux, retaining only stations with a minimum of three years
of valid daily data — the threshold required for the model to have at least two
full years of training targets after the 365-day rolling context window is
accounted for, and consistent with the quality standard enforced during ICOS and
AmeriFlux preprocessing. This criterion removed 430 short-record ISMN stations,
yielding a final dataset of 1,048 stations: 894 from ISMN (soil moisture only), 63
from ICOS (37 with collocated flux and soil moisture, 26 flux only), and 91 from
AmeriFlux (12 with collocated flux and soil moisture, 79 flux only).

These stations were partitioned into training, validation, out-of-sample (OOS), and
temporal evaluation sets using a Stratified Environmental Split (SES) framework
designed to ensure environmental representativeness while preventing spatial data
leakage. As Sentinel-2 imagery is extracted as a 264 × 264 pixel patch at 10 m
resolution (covering 2.64 × 2.64 km) centred on each station, any two stations
within 3 km share the same satellite pixels and therefore cannot belong to different
evaluation sets without introducing leakage. All 1,048 stations were therefore first
grouped using pairwise haversine distances and a Union-Find algorithm, yielding 958
location groups; 50 groups contained two or more co-located stations, of which 10
were cross-network (ISMN ↔ ICOS or ISMN ↔ AmeriFlux). All downstream partitioning
operates at the group level, guaranteeing that co-located stations always fall in
the same partition.

Each location group was assigned to one of 20 environmental cells defined by the
cross-product of five Köppen-Geiger macro-zones (A = Tropical, B = Arid,
C = Temperate, D = Continental, E = Polar) and four IGBP ecosystem macro-groups
(Forest: ENF/EBF/DNF/DBF/MF; Shrub-Savanna: SAV/OSH/CSH/WSA; Grass-Crop: GRA/CRO;
Other). Within each cell containing at least three location groups, 20% of groups
were drawn at random (seed 42) to form the OOS holdout, ensuring that all
climate-ecosystem combinations present in the full dataset are represented in the
evaluation set. Cells with fewer than three groups were assigned entirely to
training. Fifteen flux-and-soil-moisture sites — approximately 30% of the 49
available, stratified by ecosystem macro-group — were separately forced into OOS to
form a dedicated joint-evaluation set for simultaneous soil moisture and latent heat
flux assessment. A further 10% of remaining training groups, drawn by the same
stratified procedure, formed a held-out validation set used for early stopping and
hyperparameter selection. A post-hoc check confirmed that the OOS elevation
distribution (Low < 500 m, Mid 500–1500 m, High > 1500 m) deviated from the
training distribution by no more than 5 percentage points in each band.

Three temporal evaluation labels were assigned. A training station is designated
OOT-eligible if (i) its record extends past 2023-01-01, providing observations in
the fixed test window; (ii) it begins no later than 2021-12-31, ensuring at least
one complete year of pre-2023 data to populate the model's 365-day rolling context
window; and (iii) its total record spans at least three years, consistent with the
global minimum data quality threshold. An OOS station meeting criterion (i) is
designated OOST-eligible — the most demanding partition, requiring simultaneous
generalisation across unseen locations and an unseen time period. The final
partitions comprise 717 training stations, 97 validation stations, 234 OOS stations
(134 OOST-eligible; 15 joint-evaluation flux+SM sites; 17 flux-only evaluation
sites), and 422 OOT-eligible training stations whose 2023–2025 observations form
the temporal test set.

The dataset contains two categories of flux sites that differ in label
availability: 49 sites with both soil moisture and latent heat flux (flux+SM), and
105 sites with latent heat flux only (flux-only). Jointly optimising for both
targets from a random initialisation risks the sparse flux signal (49 sites) being
overwhelmed by the dense soil moisture signal (894 ISMN stations), causing the
encoder to optimise primarily for soil moisture and leaving the flux head
under-constrained. We therefore adopt a two-phase training curriculum. In Phase 1,
the model is trained on soil moisture alone across all 1,048 stations, developing
robust spatiotemporal representations of land surface conditions from a
geographically and climatically diverse dataset. In Phase 2, joint fine-tuning
introduces latent heat flux supervision: all 717 training stations continue to
contribute the soil moisture loss, while 34 flux+SM and 88 flux-only training
sites additionally contribute the LE loss, with missing labels masked per station.
This two-phase approach expands the effective LE training set from 34 to 122 sites
compared to using only flux+SM stations, while the soil moisture loss on 717
stations prevents catastrophic forgetting of the representations learned in Phase 1.
The separation of OOS flux sites into joint-evaluation (15 flux+SM) and
flux-only-evaluation (17 flux-only) subsets further enables a direct empirical
comparison of LE generalisation with and without soil moisture co-supervision,
providing an interpretable measure of the value of joint training.

ABLATION SUBSET
---------------
Every ablation study trains on the same fixed 151-station subset (ablation_train=True),
evaluated on the full 97-station val set and the 54-station OOS proxy (ablation_oos=True).
This makes all ablation comparisons controlled — the only variable is the architectural
change being tested.

  Ablation dataset size:
    ablation_train:  151 stations × ~4yr avg × 365 days ≈ 220,000 samples  (~2-4h/GPU)
    Full training:   717 stations × ~5.6yr avg × 365 days ≈ 1,470,000 samples  (days/GPU)

  Ablation subset composition:
    151 train stations  (21% of 717) — proportional KG×IGBP coverage maintained
    5 flux+SM sites in ablation_train  (Phase 2 LE signal)
    17 flux-only sites in ablation_train  (Phase 2 LE signal)
    54 OOS proxy stations  (23% of 234)

  Planned ablations (all use the same 151-station miniature):
    Encoder ablations:
      - No TerraMind          replace with random patch statistics
      - No ERA5               remove climate MLP
      - No SoilGrids          remove soil properties MLP
      + TWSA (GRACE)          add 300 km water storage anomaly token
      + SIF (TROPOMI)         add 3.5 km fluorescence token
    Temporal ablations:
      - Sequence length 365→180 days   shorter context window
      - Sequence length 365→730 days   longer context window
      - 2 transformer layers (vs 6)    shallower architecture
    Training strategy ablations:
      - Joint from scratch    no Phase 1 SM pre-training
      - SM only               no Phase 2 joint fine-tuning
      - No flux-only in Phase 2   only 34 flux+SM (vs 122) for LE

  Plots: plots/splits/09_ablation_subset.svg — full vs ablation IGBP×KG heatmap

ALGORITHM

  Step 1 — Co-location groups
    Pairwise haversine distances for all 1,048 stations.
    Union-Find groups stations within 3 km (S2 patch footprint = 2.64 km).
    Result: 958 groups; 50 with ≥2 stations; 10 cross-network.
    All split decisions made at group level — never individual station.

  Step 2 — Flux+SM joint-eval reservation
    From 49 flux+SM sites, sample 15 (~30%) stratified by IGBP macro-group (seed 42):
      Forest: 7  (ENF×5, EBF×1, DBF×1, DNF×1, MF×1)
      Grass-Crop: 4  (CRO×2, GRA×2)
      Shrub-Savanna: 2  (CSH×1, OSH×1)
      Other: 1  (WET×1)
    Entire location group forced into OOS. These sites get joint_eval = True.
    Remaining 34 flux+SM sites available for Phase 2 joint fine-tuning.

  Step 3 — Temporal eligibility
    OOT cut: 2023-01-01.
    OOT-eligible if: end_date ≥ 20230101 AND start_date ≤ 20211231 AND n_years ≥ 3.

  Steps 4–5 — Stratified OOS selection
    20-cell grid: kg_macro (5) × igbp_macro (4).
    Per cell with ≥3 groups: sample ~20% for OOS (seed 42).
    Cells with <3 groups: all to training.

  Step 5b — Validation set selection
    From remaining training groups: sample ~10% per cell (seed 42).
    Result: 97 validation stations.

  Step 6 — Elevation balance check
    If any elevation band deviates >10 pp between OOS and training:
    swap one qualifying location group (always whole group).

  Step 7 — Label assignment
    split         = oos / val / train
    oot_eligible  = (train) and temporal criteria
    oost_eligible = (oos) and end_date ≥ 2023
    joint_eval    = reserved 15 flux+SM OOS sites

  Integrity check: every location_group_id maps to exactly one split.

  Step 5c — Ablation subset selection (runs after split column exists)
    Training groups: sample ~20% per KG×IGBP cell → ablation_train (151 stations)
    OOS groups:      sample ~20% per KG×IGBP cell → ablation_oos (54 stations)
    Val (97): used unchanged for all ablations — no ablation subset needed.
    Cells with <3 groups: include all in ablation for that cell (preserve rare envs).
    Same seed 42, same co-location constraint.

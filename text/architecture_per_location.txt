================================================================================
PER-LOCATION PROCESSOR — DETAILED ARCHITECTURE, INPUT TO OUTPUT
Written 2026-08-17.  Designed, not built.
================================================================================

WHAT THE MODEL DOES
  One sample = one station, one target date.  It reads a 2.24 km x 2.24 km tile
  centred on that station (satellite imagery history, terrain, soil, weather)
  and predicts volumetric soil moisture at 3 depth bins (0-10, 10-30, 30-50 cm)
  for that date, at every location in the tile.  Supervision is ONE point
  sensor.

WHAT IS BEING FIXED
  Today all time-series signal (ERA5 365 days, S2/S1 history, SIF, TWSA) reaches
  the decoder through ONE SPATIALLY CONSTANT VECTOR — `context` at
  model.py:736-743, applied by FiLM as a uniform per-channel scale/shift.
  Consequence: the spatial pattern can change between samples, but the RESPONSE
  TO WEATHER cannot vary across the tile.  Two stations 500 m apart can have
  different means, never different response functions.

  Measured signature (§26.11, §23):
    within-station temporal SD  0.051  vs observed 0.051   <- essentially perfect
    between-station SD          15-19% of observed         <- the failure
    r(pred level, obs level) at CR200-18   -0.175          <- ANTI-correlated
    norm-std across tiles       0.093-0.524, strongest where terrain is flattest

  The delta of this design: the model already has S (196 per-location tokens,
  model.py:731-734) and g (`context`).  Nothing ever combines A SPECIFIC
  LOCATION's context with THAT LOCATION's history.  Block 3 below is that, and
  it is the entire architectural change.  Everything else is inputs to it.

  s1_doys/valid/rel_pos (40,)       long/bool/long

  WHY s2_tok_k REPLACES s2_pyr (60,4,768):
    _cpu_pyramid_pool (dataset.py:190-220) collapses (M,196,768) into 4
    concentric-crop scales and destroys 97-98% of position information (§27a.2).
    Indexing column k BEFORE pooling preserves position AND halves the payload:
      60 x 768        =  92 KB      <- one location's own history column
      60 x 4 x 768    = 184 KB      <- the 4-scale pyramid it replaces
    The ~30 MB/sample IPC blowup that _cpu_pyramid_pool was built to prevent
    only occurs if all 196 columns ship.  Training needs one.

1.2 TERRAIN [*]  (TWI, HAND — see PART 2 for how they are produced)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  terrain_lo        [*] (3, 28, 28)   fp32   80 m grid  -> Block 1
  terrain_hi        [*] (3, 224, 224) fp16   30 m info  -> Block 4

  Channels in both: [ TWI , HAND , valid_mask ]

  valid_mask IS A CHANNEL, not merely a filter, so the network can tell
  "imputed" from "average terrain".  Invalid cells (water, no-data, stations
  that failed the boundary QC) are set to 0 AFTER standardisation — i.e. the
  channel mean — and the mask channel says so.
  HAND is log1p'd before standardising (heavy-tailed in metres).  TWI is
  already a log, so it is roughly symmetric and needs no transform.

1.3 FINE RASTERS FOR THE PER-PIXEL HEAD [*]  (all measured, none learned)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  s1_hi   [*] (5, 224, 224) fp16   vv_now, vh_now, vv_med, vh_med, days_since
  s2_hi   [*] (3, 224, 224) fp16   ndvi, swir_index, days_since
  lulc_hi [*] (2, 224, 224) fp16   learned 2-d embedding of the LULC class
  soil_hi [*] (3, 224, 224) fp16   soc, socd, bd — nearest from 74x74 @ 30 m

  S1 IS FED AS (latest, long-term median) DELIBERATELY.
    current minus baseline IS the wetness anomaly; the baseline alone is
    roughness + vegetation.  Letting the model difference them itself is
    strictly more expressive than feeding either one.

  days_since IS REQUIRED.  "Latest clear" has variable staleness; without it,
  three-week-old imagery reads as current.  Reuse the staleness embedding
  already at model.py:505-514.

  SPECKLE MUST BE HANDLED (multi-look or a short temporal median).  S1's 0.76
  sub-token variance fraction (§30.3a) partly IS speckle, which is sub-token
  noise by definition — so 0.76 is an upper bound and unhandled speckle turns
  the added detail into variance rather than signal.

  ELEVATION IS EXCLUDED from Block 4.  Measured sub-token fraction 0.05: the
  160 m token already carries essentially all of it, so including it at 10 m is
  pure redundancy.  It stays in Block 1.

PART 2 — PRODUCING TWI AND HAND
================================================================================

2.1 WHY THE CURRENT 2.24 km TILE IS NOT ENOUGH  (measured 2026-08-17)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Both quantities funnel through FLOW ACCUMULATION, which is non-local.
  Sampled 30 tiles from satellite_zarr:
      lowest cell lies ON THE TILE BOUNDARY in 25 of 30  = 83.3%
      relief: median 188 m, range 13.5-797 m
  Flow leaves the window.  The channel a station drains to is outside it.

  TWI:  the error would be ALIGNED WITH THE SIGNAL.  A valley cell that really
        drains 50 km2 gets only what leaks in from the tile edge, so the
        underestimate is largest exactly where TWI should be HIGH.  Not noise —
        a bias that flattens the very contrast being measured.  §23 again.
  HAND: often UNDEFINED, not merely inaccurate.  A 2.24 km headwater tile may
        hold no cell above any sensible stream threshold.  Lower the threshold
        until one qualifies and you have invented a stream; HAND then degenerates
        to "height above the lowest cell in the box" = local relief renamed.

2.2 THE RECIPE
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    0. hydro-condition FIRST      breach (preferred) or fill depressions
    1. flow direction             MFD/D-inf for accumulation
                                  D8        for the HAND trace     <- BOTH
    2. flow accumulation  A
    3. a = A_cells * cellsize     specific catchment area [m]
    4. beta = Horn(dem)           s=1 — the wide DEM is fetched at NATIVE 30 m
    5. TWI  = ln( a / max(tan beta, 1e-3) )
    6. streams = A > threshold    threshold calibrated against MERIT hnd ~ 0
    7. HAND = elev - elev(first stream cell reached by the D8 trace)

  FOUR THINGS THE BARE RECIPE NEEDS:
    (i)   HAND NEEDS D8 EVEN THOUGH TWI NEEDS MFD.  Under MFD flow splits across
          several downslope neighbours, so there is no single "the stream cell it
          drains to" to trace.  Two flow-direction products from one conditioned
          DEM.  pyflwdir and WhiteboxTools both do exactly this.
    (ii)  CONDITION ON A BUFFER, THEN CROP.  A window edge otherwise acts as a
          wall: flow that should exit ponds, and depressions touching the edge
          fill to spurious levels.
    (iii) ASSERT THE ACCUMULATION UNIT.  Libraries return cell COUNTS or AREA.
          a = n*cellsize is the count version.  A factor of 30 inside a log is a
          silent constant offset that nothing downstream will catch.
    (iv)  SLOPE FLOOR 1e-3 (0.057 deg) and A FLOORED AT ONE CELL AREA.  TxSON
          mean slope is 2.5-4.1 deg so near-flat cells are common and
          tan beta -> 0 sends TWI to +inf.  REPORT THE PER-TILE FRACTION HITTING
          THE FLOOR — a large fraction means TWI is degenerate there, not clipped.

2.3 WINDOW SIZE — the load-bearing choice
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  KEY REFINEMENT: the non-local error is LARGELY COMMON-MODE within a tile.
  The model uses within-tile contrast:
        dTWI = d ln(a) - d ln(tan beta)
  Two cells on the same hillslope both have small local catchments and neither
  receives the upstream inflow, so a truncated window gets their DIFFERENCE
  right while getting both absolute values wrong.  A tile-constant offset is
  removed by standardisation anyway.

  It breaks specifically at CHANNEL CELLS, which receive the outside inflow
  while adjacent hillslope cells do not.  Hence a checkable criterion:

      A TILE IS AT RISK IFF IT CONTAINS A CHANNEL
      — read directly from MERIT `upa` inside the 2.24 km tile.

  Headwater tiles are computable from a modest window with the contrast
  essentially correct.

  BUT `a` STILL MATTERS WITHIN A TILE — it cannot be left at 90 m.
  Order of magnitude across one hillslope:
      a    ~30 m at the top  ->  ~500 m near the base   =>  d ln(a)       ~ 2.8
      tanb  0.02             ->   0.10                  =>  d ln(tan b)   ~ 1.6
  The `a` term carries AT LEAST AS MUCH within-tile contrast as the slope term.

  PROPOSED WINDOW: 10 km at 30 m = 333^2 cells ~ 0.44 MB/station,
                   ~440 MB for all 993.  Sized up per station where MERIT `upa`
                   says the catchment is larger.
  Compute and storage were never the constraint.

2.4 MERIT'S ROLE — VALIDATOR AND CALIBRATOR, NOT PRODUCT
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    upa INSIDE the tile        -> does this tile contain a channel? (2.3)
    upa AT the station         -> how wide the window must be
    our accumulation vs MERIT  -> PER-STATION BOUNDARY-CAPTURE PASS/FAIL,
                                  with ground truth
    hnd ~ 0 cells              -> calibrates our stream threshold instead of us
                                  picking it by hand
    —                          -> fallback product where no window is practical
  ~10 KB per station, and it makes every downstream choice measurable rather
  than assumed.

2.5 THE LARGEST CORRECTNESS RISK: GLO-30 IS A DSM
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Copernicus GLO-30 includes CANOPY AND BUILDINGS.  A 20 m tree line dams a
  stream, and conditioning then routes flow around a ridge that does not exist.
  This is precisely why MERIT Hydro exists as a separate product — it removed
  absolute bias, stripes, speckle AND tree height.

  Prefer FABDEM (GLO-30 with forest and buildings removed; CC-BY-NC, fine for
  research — CONFIRM THE ACCESS ROUTE, do not assume it).
  Failing that, BREACH rather than fill (whitebox BreachDepressionsLeastCost, or
  richdem Priority-Flood): breaching carves through obstructions instead of
  drowning them in flat filled areas, and behaves markedly better under canopy.

  NONE of pyflwdir / richdem / whitebox / pysheds is installed in either env
  (verified).  pyflwdir (Deltares) is the best single fit: flow direction,
  accumulation and hand() in one numba-backed library, developed against MERIT
  so the conventions match.

2.6 THEORETICAL BASIS — AND WHY IT DICTATES BLOCK 3
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TWI is TOPMODEL (Beven & Kirkby 1979).  Assuming the water table parallels
  the surface, steady state, and T = T0*exp(-f*z):
        R*a = T0 exp(-f z) tan beta
          z = (1/f) [ ln(T0/R) - ln(a/tan beta) ]
            = (1/f) [ ln(T0/R) - TWI ]
  DEPTH TO WATER TABLE IS LINEAR IN -TWI.  That is the entire theoretical
  content: TWI is a WATER-TABLE-DEPTH index.

  HAND (Renno et al. 2008; Nobre et al. 2011) approximates the same quantity
  more directly — the stream is the local hydraulic datum — with no steady-state,
  recharge or transmissivity assumption.  It is also numerically more robust: a
  height difference, whereas TWI's `a` is very sensitive to the flow algorithm.

  NEITHER PREDICTS *SURFACE* MOISTURE except through water-table coupling.
  Capillary rise: ~0.1-0.3 m sand, ~1-2 m silt, 3-10 m clay.  Once HAND exceeds
  a few metres the top 10 cm is hydraulically DECOUPLED and is governed instead
  by infiltration, evaporative demand and the retention curve — a 1-D VERTICAL
  process, not TOPMODEL's lateral one.  TxSON has 46-88 m relief.

  THE EMPIRICAL RESULT THAT MATTERS MOST — Grayson et al. (1997), and the
  Tarrawarra work of Western, Grayson & Bloschl: terrain control of surface soil
  moisture is STATE-DEPENDENT.
      WET state -> lateral flow organises the pattern, terrain explains much of
                   the variance                          ("nonlocal control")
      DRY state -> soil and vegetation dominate, terrain explains ~nothing
                                                          ("local control")

  ==> THIS IS WHY THE ARCHITECTURE BELOW IS THE RIGHT ONE.
      If the effect is state-dependent, feeding TWI as a static input that
      shifts a mean is the WRONG FUNCTIONAL FORM — it fits the average of two
      regimes and matches neither.  What is required is terrain INTERACTED WITH
      WETNESS STATE.  That is exactly what Block 3's cross-attention provides,
      and exactly what a uniform FiLM scale/shift structurally cannot express.

  TESTABLE PREDICTIONS this generates — terrain should explain dSM better:
      in WET periods than dry           (sharpest; split by antecedent precip)
      at 30-50 cm than at 0-10 cm       (deeper is closer to saturation deficit)
      where HAND is SMALL               (metres, not tens of metres)
      in humid low-relief settings      (vs semi-arid)
  If dSM is flat across all four splits the mechanism is absent, and no DEM
  resolution rescues it.

  THE COMPETING MECHANISM, STATED FAIRLY.  If lateral flow does not reach
  0-10 cm, heterogeneity still comes from somewhere — Grayson's "local controls":
  texture, vegetation, aspect-driven insolation.  §30.3a measured these varying
  within our tiles (texture 0.26-0.29, socd/soc 0.50-0.54, northness 0.51), and
  CR200-18's full-tile range is 11 wt% clay / 22.75 wt% sand.  TWI and HAND
  address ONLY ONE of the two candidate mechanisms.

PART 3 — BLOCK 1: CONTEXT ENCODER   (runs ONCE per sample, whole tile)

3.1 TERRAIN STEM [*]
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    terrain_lo (B,3,28,28)
      -> Conv2d(3, 768, k=3, stride=2, pad=1)     28x28 -> 14x14
      -> GELU
      -> Conv2d(768, 768, k=1)                    <- ZERO-INIT weight AND bias
      -> reshape                                  (B, 196, 768) = terrain_tokens

  A LEARNED stem, not hand-picked pooling: it decides for itself how to reduce
  80 m -> 160 m.  stride-2 because 28 is exactly 2x14, so there is no resampling
  ambiguity anywhere in the path.

3.2 WHERE TERRAIN IS ADDED
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  At model.py:501-505, where spatial_pe is already applied:

    spatial_tokens = anchor_l12 + spatial_pe + modality_emb + terrain_tokens
                                                              ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ NEW

  THREE CONSEQUENCES, EACH LOAD-BEARING:

  (a) BIT-IDENTITY IS FREE.  With the final 1x1 zero-initialised,
      terrain_tokens == 0 at init.  No sequence-length change, no new token, no
      perturbed attention => the forward pass is EXACTLY best.pt's.  This is
      what makes terrain a safe additive change rather than a rewrite, and it is
      what the regression gate checks.

  (b) PER-LOCATION ALIGNMENT.  Added INTO the 196 spatial tokens, so token k's
      terrain lands on token k.  Appending 4 pooled terrain tokens the way
      dem_pyr / lulc_pyr are handled would make terrain TILE-LEVEL, able only to
      shift the mean — the exact failure being fixed.

  (c) NO EXTRA TOKEN IS NEEDED FOR g.  `context` is computed from the encoder
      OUTPUT (model.py:736-743), so the non-spatial tokens have already attended
      over the spatial tokens by then.  Terrain reaches g through attention.

3.3 SEQUENCE ASSEMBLY  (model.py:532-666, order and length unchanged)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   idx      block                     tokens   built from
   -----    ----------------------    ------   ----------------------------------
   0-3      DEM pyramid                  4     dem_pyr + scale_emb + static_mod[0]
   4-7      LULC pyramid                 4     lulc_pyr + scale_emb + static_mod[1]
   8-11     Soil                         4     SoilEncoder(21,74,74) (model.py:287)
   12-207   SPATIAL  <- spatial_start  196     anchor_l12 + spatial_pe
                                               + spatial_modality_emb(anchor_orbit)
                                               + staleness(anchor_rel_pos)
                                               + terrain_tokens              [*]
   ...      S2 history                 240     60 acq x 4 scales  (-> 60 with [*])
   ...      S1 history                 160     40 acq x 4 scales  (-> 40 with [*])
   ...      ERA5                       365     one token per day
   ...      SIF                      <= 50
   ...      TWSA                     <= 12
                                    -------
                                    <= 1035 tokens @ d_model = 768

  Depth-CLS tokens (use_cls_depth) are prepended; `depth_offset` shifts
  spatial_start accordingly, so downstream code uses
  sp_start = spatial_start + depth_offset.

  POSITION EMBEDDING for the 196 spatial tokens (model.py:403-404, 501-505):
      spatial_row_emb = nn.Embedding(14, 768)
      spatial_col_emb = nn.Embedding(14, 768)
      spatial_pe = (row_emb[r].unsqueeze(1) + col_emb[c].unsqueeze(0))
                     .reshape(196, 768)
  A SEPARABLE 2-D additive embedding.  MUST BE INDEXED BY ABSOLUTE ROW/COL, not
  crop-relative — see 3.5.

3.4 ENCODER AND OUTPUTS

    S = ctx[:, sp_start : sp_start+196, :]        (B, 196, 768)

    g = masked mean of ctx over all NON-spatial, non-CLS, non-pad tokens
                                                  (B, 768)
        The tile summary.  This is today's `context`, unchanged.
        NOTE it includes the pooled DEM/LULC/soil tokens at indices 0-11 — the
        code comment calling it "temporal context" is imprecise.

PART 5 — BLOCK 3: PER-LOCATION PROCESSOR   <<< THE ARCHITECTURAL DELTA >>>
         (per location k, weights SHARED across all k)
================================================================================

    seq_k = [ S[:,k,:] ,   g   ,  s2_tok_k (60) ,  s1_tok_k (40) ]
              varies      const     varies           varies
                                                    ~102 tokens

    L x (
          self-attention over seq_k
          cross-attention:  query = seq_k,  key/value = W      <-- INTERACTION
        )

    h_k  (B, 768)   ->  linear  ->  SM(3 depths) at location k

  WHAT VARIES WITH k — THE WHOLE MECHANISM:
    S[:,k,:]     own context, now carrying TWI/HAND    YES
    s2_tok_k     own S2 history column                 YES
    s1_tok_k     own S1 history column                 YES
    g            tile summary                          no
    W            weather                               no

  The encoders run ONCE; only the SLICE differs.  If everything fed to the
  processor were tile-constant, every location would emit an identical
  trajectory and today's failure would be rebuilt with more machinery.

  THE FIX IS NOT PER-LOCATION INPUTS, IT IS PER-LOCATION *TEMPORAL* INPUTS.
  Static terrain alone can only shift a location's mean.  What lets two
  locations respond differently to the same rain is that each carries its own
  S2/S1 HISTORY COLUMN, read through its own terrain context.

  THE CROSS-ATTENTION IS WHERE THE PHYSICS LIVES.  A query derived from S_k —
  which now encodes "this location is a hollow, TWI 12, HAND 3 m" — can attend
  differently to the weather sequence than a ridge's query does.  A hollow can
  learn "after three days of rain I stay wet a week longer"; a ridge learns
  "I dry in a day".  Per 2.6, that state-dependent interaction IS the effect.

  TERRAIN IS NOT FED INTO seq_k SEPARATELY.  It is already inside S_k; re-adding
  it is parameters for no new information.

  WHY ONE-PIXEL SUPERVISION SUFFICES.  The processor is ONE SHARED function
  f(local context, weather) -> SM(t), fitted at supervised locations across 993
  STATIONS spanning a huge context range.  Running it at an unsupervised
  location inside a tile is INTERPOLATION IN CONTEXT SPACE, not extrapolation.

  COST:
                            training        inference
    Block 1 context         full tile, once      once
    Block 2 weather         once                 once
    Block 3 processor       1 LOCATION           196
    Block 4 per-pixel head  1 PIXEL              50,176 (one batched 1x1 conv)

6.1 THE RULE THAT MAKES A 10 m OUTPUT DEFENSIBLE
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The criterion was never resolution.  It is WHERE SUB-TOKEN DETAIL COMES FROM:

                        | 14x14 out | unpatchify   | token gather + rasters
    --------------------|-----------|--------------|------------------------
    output grid         |   160 m   |    10 m      |        10 m
    sub-token detail    |     —     | token embed  |   MEASURED PIXELS
    defensible under    |    yes    |     NO       |        YES
    1 label / 50,176 px |           |              |

  Unpatchify is rejected because the network would INVENT sub-token structure
  with nothing to check it — §17.7's objection to the DiVAE, and what §23 caught
  the decoder actually doing.  A nearest gather of the 160 m token concatenated
  with MEASURED 10-30 m rasters invents nothing: every sub-token variation
  traces to a measurement.

6.2 NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * NEAREST GATHER, NEVER BILINEAR.
  * BLOCKINESS IS A DIAGNOSTIC, NOT A DEFECT.  If the fine rasters are inert,
    the output shows visible 16-px blocking.  That IS the signal that Block 4 is
    doing nothing.  Bilinear hides exactly that failure behind a smooth ramp —
    which is how the current map looks plausible while being anti-correlated
    with the landscape.  DO NOT SMOOTH IT AWAY.  REPORT IT.
  * NO WITHIN-BLOCK COORDINATE FEATURE (i%16, j%16).  It would let the model
    paint arbitrary sub-token patterns from position alone, i.e. invent
    structure, which is the whole thing being avoided.
  * EVERY FINE CHANNEL MUST BE MEASURED.  No learned upsampling anywhere.

6.3 UNetDecoder IS DELETED
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  model.py:188 — four upsampling stages at 512/256/128/64 plus three FiLM'd
  skips (L9/L6/L3), all modulated by the spatially-constant `context`.  Kept
  runnable behind --head unet for the regression gate and rollback.
  §28.2's figure: ~3.2 M activations/sample/depth against ~200 K here.
  TRAINING GETS CHEAPER, not more expensive.

  WHY TERRAIN IS ADDED, NOT APPENDED.  Three reasons, each load-bearing:
    1. BIT-IDENTITY.  terrain_tokens == 0 at init, and adding zero changes nothing.  No new
       token, no sequence-length change, no perturbed attention → the forward pass is EXACTLY
       best.pt's.  Appending a token would change the sequence and break the regression gate.
    2. ALIGNMENT.  Token k's terrain must land on token k.  Appending 4 POOLED terrain tokens
       (the dem_pyr / lulc_pyr pattern) would make terrain TILE-LEVEL — able only to shift the
       mean, which is the exact failure being fixed.
    3. g GETS IT ANYWAY.  g is computed from the encoder OUTPUT, so the non-spatial tokens have
       already attended over the spatial tokens.  Terrain reaches g through attention; no extra
       token is required.

  EFFECTIVE RESOLUTION IS CHANNEL-DEPENDENT — ~10 m where S1/S2 drive the
  prediction, ~30 m where terrain does, 160 m elsewhere.

                       cells per token    cells per tile
      MERIT 90 m             ~3               25x25
      own 30 m              ~28               75x75
  At ~3 samples you are POINT-SAMPLING MERIT, not averaging it.  At ~28 you get
  a real block statistic — and that is where the tan(beta) and HAND
  NONLINEARITIES are handled correctly, since derive-fine-then-average is NOT
  equal to derive-coarse.

  AGGREGATE BEFORE THE NONLINEARITY.  Average tan(beta) and HAND over each 80 m
  block BEFORE the log/ratio, never after.

  A COMPOSITE IS ONLY AS FINE AS ITS COARSEST INGREDIENT.  If `a` came from
  MERIT at 90 m while beta came from our tile at 30 m, TWI would be 90 m with a
  better-estimated slope term — NOT 30 m TWI.  This is why `a` is recomputed at
  30 m rather than taken from MERIT.

  §23 measured 64-83% of variance already sitting at 14x14.  REPORT 14x14 AND
  224 SEPARATELY: if 224 helps only via the static branch, that is a
  resolution-limited offset, not resolved dynamics, and §23 is explicit the
  distinction must not be blurred.

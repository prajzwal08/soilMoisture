PART 1 — SUMMARY
================================================================================

1.1  WHAT THE MODEL DOES

One sample is one station on one date.  The model reads a 2.24 km x 2.24 km tile
centred on that station — satellite image history, terrain, soil, a year of
weather — and predicts volumetric soil moisture at three depths (0-10, 10-30,
30-50 cm) for that date, at every location in the tile.

It is trained against ONE point sensor.  That single fact drives every design
decision below.


1.2  WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE CURRENT MODEL

It gets time almost perfectly right and space almost entirely wrong.

    within-station temporal SD    0.051  against observed 0.051    <- near perfect
    between-station SD            15-19% of observed               <- the failure
    r(predicted level, observed level) at CR200-18    -0.175       <- BACKWARDS
    norm-std across tiles         0.093-0.524, and highest at the FLATTEST site

The last two lines matter more than the second.  The map is not merely flat; it
is wrong, and it is most confidently wrong where the landscape is most uniform.
The model is painting variation that has nothing to do with the ground.

The cause is one line of code.  At model.py:736-743 a vector called `context` is
built as the masked mean of every non-spatial token, and FiLM applies it to the
decoder as a uniform per-channel scale and shift.  So the ENTIRE time series —
365 days of ERA5, all the S2 and S1 history, SIF, TWSA — reaches the output
through a single vector that is the same everywhere in the tile.

The consequence is precise: the spatial pattern may change from sample to sample,
but the RESPONSE TO WEATHER cannot vary across the tile.  Two stations 500 m
apart can be assigned different mean values.  They cannot be assigned different
behaviour.  A hollow that drains slowly and a ridge that dries in a day are, to
this model, the same place with different offsets.

That is exactly the observed signature: temporal variation right, spatial
variation absent or inverted.


1.3  WHAT THE FIX IS

The model already computes almost everything needed.  It has 196 per-location
token vectors (model.py:731-734).  It has a tile summary (`context`).  What it
has never had is anything that combines A PARTICULAR LOCATION's context with THAT
LOCATION's history.

So we split the model into four blocks:

  BLOCK 1  encodes the tile once, producing one context vector per location
  BLOCK 2  encodes the weather once, shared by every location
  BLOCK 3  runs a small transformer PER LOCATION, attending over that location's
           own context and its own image history, cross-attending into weather
  BLOCK 4  turns each location's output into a map by combining it with MEASURED
           fine-resolution channels at each 32 m cell

Block 3 is the whole change.  Blocks 1, 2 and 4 exist to feed it or to read it.

The reason this works is that Block 3 sees things that differ between locations —
S_k, and that location's own S2 and S1 history columns — while weather and the
tile summary stay shared.  So the same shared function, given the same weather,
can produce different trajectories at different places.  That is the capability
the current model structurally lacks.

Crucially: the fix is not per-location INPUTS, it is per-location TEMPORAL
inputs.  Static terrain can only shift a mean.  What lets two places respond
differently to the same rain is that each carries its own image history.


1.4  THE DECODER IS GONE

The current model upsamples 14x14 -> 224x224 with a U-Net.  We remove it, for two
reasons.

First, it has nowhere to get fine detail from.  A CNN U-Net works because its
encoder downsamples 224->112->56->28->14 and the skip connections carry genuine
high-resolution features.  TerraMind is a Vision Transformer; it does not
downsample.  L3, L6, L9 and L12 are ALL at 14x14 (model.py:530).  So the decoder
was upsampling a 14x14 bottleneck using skips that were themselves 14x14,
interpolated up (model.py:258,261,264).  There was never any 10 m information in
that path.

Second, we already tested it under both supervision regimes without meaning to.
Given DENSE targets (§16.4) it reproduced structure almost perfectly — norm-std
0.0061 -> 0.2504, correlation 1.000.  Given ONE label per tile (§23) it produced
maps anti-correlated with the terrain.  Same decoder.  The problem was never the
decoder; it was asking a generative upsampler to invent 50,176 values from one
measurement.

In its place, each output cell is computed independently: look up the token that
covers it, attach the measured channels at that cell, run a small shared MLP.
Nothing upsamples.  If the measured channels carry nothing, the output shows
visible 5x5 blocks — which is the map telling you so.  A decoder would have
smoothed that into a plausible gradient and told you nothing.


1.5  THE OUTPUT IS 70x70 AT 32 m

Not 10 m, and not 30 m.

Not 10 m because after the token gather the finest real carriers are S1/S2/LULC
at 10 m but terrain and soil at 30 m, so most of a 10 m grid would be
interpolation.  Not 30 m because 2240/30 = 74.67 — it does not divide the tile,
and each token would straddle a ragged 5.33 cells.

32 m divides exactly: 2240/32 = 70 cells, and 70/14 = 5.  Every token covers
precisely a 5x5 block, the gather is an integer divide, and there is no ragged
edge anywhere.  32 m is 6.7% coarser than 30 m, which is nothing when the thermal
band is 100 m.

This also makes the resolution claim honest without an asterisk: at 32 m, every
channel feeding the output is at or below the output resolution.


1.6  TERRAIN: TWI AND HAND, COMPUTED OURSELVES

Two hydrological descriptors enter at Block 1 so that terrain can modify the
RESPONSE to weather rather than paint an offset on the output.

  TWI   ln(a / tan beta)     high in convergent hollows, low on ridges
  HAND  height above the nearest drainage, following the flow path

Both depend on flow accumulation, which is non-local: it needs everything
upslope.  Our tile does not contain it.  Measured on 30 sampled tiles, THE LOWEST
CELL LIES ON THE TILE BOUNDARY IN 25 OF 30 (83.3%) — water leaves the box, so the
stream a station drains to is outside it.

So we fetch a wider DEM at native 30 m, condition it, and route flow ourselves.
MERIT Hydro is used as the REFEREE rather than the source: its upslope area tells
us how wide each station's window must be, its value at the station tells us
whether our window was wide enough, and its drainage network calibrates our
stream threshold so we are not inventing one.


1.7  LST AS AN AUXILIARY TARGET

Block 1 currently receives feedback about one location per sample.  We attach a
second head to its 196 output vectors predicting the within-tile land surface
temperature anomaly, giving it a few hundred supervised values per sample instead
of one.

This is NOT because temperature predicts soil moisture.  We tested that in §29
and it does not: within-station correlation is -0.077, and the apparent +0.167
across stations is a Simpson's-paradox artefact of station identity.  The
auxiliary is about SUPERVISION DENSITY, not correlation.

It is a side branch.  Nothing downstream reads it; it only produces gradient.


1.8  WHAT COULD MAKE ALL OF THIS POINTLESS, STATED UP FRONT

TWI and HAND are saturation-excess concepts.  They predict where the water table
is shallow.  Our labels are the top 10 cm, which in most settings is governed by
rain and evaporation, not lateral flow — and once HAND exceeds a few metres the
surface is hydraulically decoupled from the water table entirely.

There is an empirical result that says exactly when terrain does and does not
work (Grayson et al. 1997): terrain organises surface moisture when the landscape
is WET, and explains almost nothing when it is DRY.

So before any of this is built, we run a gate: does terrain actually predict the
observed difference in soil moisture between our 62 pairs of nearby stations,
split by wet and dry?  It costs an afternoon and no GPU.  §29 was this same gate
and it killed that arm before anyone wrote model code.

If the gate fails, we lose one input — not the architecture.  Block 3 works
through per-location image history, which has nothing to do with terrain.

2.1  SYMBOLS

  B             batch size
  d_model=768   width of every token vector, everywhere

  k             A LOCATION INDEX, 0..195 — one of the 196 tokens in the tile.
                Training supervises ONE k per sample; inference runs all 196.
  i, j          an OUTPUT CELL index, 0..69, on the 70x70 grid at 32 m

  ctx           the full transformer output, <=1035 tokens.  Block 1's raw
                output, before anything is sliced from it.
  S             (B,196,768)  PER-LOCATION CONTEXT.  ctx sliced at the 196 spatial
                positions.  S[:,k,:] describes location k: how it looks, its
                terrain, and what it absorbed from the rest of the tile through
                six layers of attention.
  S_k           shorthand for S[:,k,:] — one location's own 768-vector
  g             (B,768)  TILE SUMMARY.  Masked mean of ctx over every valid
                non-spatial, non-CLS, non-pad token.  Same for every k.
                This is today's `context` (model.py:736-743), unchanged.
  W             (B,T_w,768)  ENCODED WEATHER — ERA5 + SIF + TWSA.  Computed once
                and SHARED.  Never replicated per location.
  seq_k         the ~102-token sequence Block 3 attends over for location k:
                [ S_k | g | s2_tok_k | s1_tok_k ]
  h_k           (B,768)  Block 3's output for location k.  Still 160 m.  The last
                thing the transformer stack produces.

  fine[:,i,j]   the C=17 MEASURED channels at output cell (i,j).  THIS, not the
                transformer, is where sub-160 m detail comes from.
  terrain_lo    (3,28,28)   TWI, HAND, valid_mask at 80 m   -> Block 1 stem
  terrain_hi    (3,70,70)   the same three at 32 m          -> Block 4
  s2_tok_k      (60,768)    location k's OWN S2 history column, all dates
  s1_tok_k      (40,768)    location k's OWN S1 history column
  pixel_idx     (2,)  (i,j) of the supervised station on the 70x70 grid
  token_idx     ()    k of the supervised station = (row//5)*14 + col//5

  a             specific catchment area [m] — the NON-LOCAL half of TWI
  beta          local slope angle — the LOCAL half of TWI
  lambda        weight on the LST auxiliary loss (Part 10)


2.2  GRIDS, AND HOW THEY NEST

  grid              cells       cell size   note
  ----------------  ----------  ----------  -----------------------------------
  raw imagery        224 x 224      10 m    S1, S2, LULC native
  TOKEN GRID          14 x 14      160 m    TerraMind patch — A HARD FLOOR
  OUTPUT              70 x 70       32 m    EXACTLY 5x5 cells per token
  soil                74 x 74       30 m    OpenLandMap native
  thermal (LST)      ~22 x 22      100 m    Landsat TIRS native.  The ST product
                                            ships at 30 m but the INFORMATION is
                                            100 m.

  THE ONE SENTENCE THAT EXPLAINS THE WHOLE DESIGN:

      The transformer says what is happening in each 160 m neighbourhood.
      The measured channels say how this particular 32 m cell differs from it.

  All 25 cells inside one token receive the IDENTICAL h_k.  They can differ ONLY
  through `fine`.  Nothing upsamples.  Nothing is invented.


2.3  DIAGRAM NOTATION USED IN PART 3

   ┌────────┐   a tensor, or a store on disk.  Shape printed inside.
   └────────┘
   ╔════════╗   a NEW or CHANGED block.  Everything else exists today.
   ╚════════╝
      │  ▼      solid arrow — a tensor flows along it, once per sample
      ┆  ▽      dashed arrow — SHARED / broadcast; computed once, read many times
      (+)       element-wise addition, NOT concatenation

READ THE THREE ARROWS INTO BLOCK 3.  S_k varies with location, the histories vary
with location, g and W do not.  The encoders run ONCE; only the SLICE differs.
If everything entering the processor were tile-constant, every location would
emit an identical trajectory and today's failure would be rebuilt with more
machinery.

THE DASHED ARROW IS THE POINT.  W is computed once and read by every location,
but each location queries it with its OWN S_k — which carries that location's TWI
and HAND.  That is the only place in the entire graph where terrain can change
the RESPONSE to weather rather than add an offset.  In the current model this
arrow does not exist: `context` is one vector applied uniformly by FiLM.

3.5  THE LST BRANCH — IT ONLY MAKES GRADIENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                    transformer — 6 layers of self-attention
                                   │
                                  ctx
                       ┌───────────┴───────────┐
                       │                       │
                S (B, 196, 768)           g (B, 768)
                       │
           ┌───────────┴────────────┐
           │                        │
     SIDE BRANCH                MAIN PATH
           │                        │
     Linear(768 → 1)           S[:, k, :]  ← pick the supervised location
           │                        │
     (B,196) → (14,14)          Block 3 → h_k
           │                        │
           │                    Block 4 → 70x70 map
           │                        │
     loss_LST                   loss_SM
     over 196 tokens            at 1 cell
           │                        │
           └──────────┬─────────────┘
                      ▼
             L = loss_SM + lambda · loss_LST

NOTHING DOWNSTREAM READS THE LST PREDICTION.  Block 3 does not see it, Block 4
does not see it, the soil moisture map does not see it.  It produces one number
that enters the total loss, and that is all.

What it does is change the GRADIENTS.  Backpropagating loss_LST enters at S and
flows backwards through the six transformer layers, into the token embeddings and
into the terrain stem.  It never flows into Block 3 or Block 4, because it never
passed through them.

Which is the point: it tells Block 1 that its 196 location vectors had better
encode something real about how this tile varies in space, because 196 of them
get checked against measured temperature every step.  Without it, Block 1 only
ever hears about one location per sample, filtered through everything downstream.

At inference the branch is simply not computed.

   └────────┬─────────┘                      └────────┬─────────┘
   ┌────────▼─────────┐                      ┌────────▼─────────┐
   │ BLOCK 4  ★ 1 cell│  (i,j) = pixel_idx   │ BLOCK 4  4,900   │  one 1x1 conv

Blocks 1 and 2 are IDENTICAL in both paths.  That is what makes training cheap —

WHY ONE-CELL SUPERVISION SUFFICES.  Block 3 is ONE SHARED function

location inside a tile is INTERPOLATION IN CONTEXT SPACE, not extrapolation.  The
training signal for within-tile variation comes from BETWEEN-station variation
across the whole dataset.

PART 5 — PRODUCING TWI AND HAND

5.1  WHY THE CURRENT 2.24 km TILE IS NOT ENOUGH   (measured 2026-08-17)

  Both quantities funnel through FLOW ACCUMULATION, which is non-local.
  Sampled 30 tiles from satellite_zarr:

      lowest cell lies ON THE TILE BOUNDARY in 25 of 30  =  83.3%
      relief: median 188 m, range 13.5-797 m

  Flow leaves the window.  The channel a station drains to is outside it.

  TWI:  the error would be ALIGNED WITH THE SIGNAL.  A valley cell that really
        drains 50 km2 receives only what leaks in from the tile edge, so the
        underestimate is largest exactly where TWI should be HIGH.  Not noise —
        a bias that flattens the very contrast being measured, and that would
        look entirely plausible.  §23 all over again.

  HAND: often UNDEFINED, not merely inaccurate.  A 2.24 km headwater tile may
        contain no cell above any sensible stream threshold.  Lower the threshold
        until one qualifies and you have invented a stream; HAND then degenerates
        into "height above the lowest cell in the box" — local relief renamed.


5.2  THE RECIPE

    0. hydro-condition FIRST     breach (preferred) or fill depressions
    1. flow direction            MFD/D-inf for accumulation
                                 D8        for the HAND trace      <- BOTH
    2. flow accumulation  A
    3. a = A_cells * cellsize    specific catchment area [m]
    4. beta = Horn(dem)          s=1 — the wide DEM is fetched at NATIVE 30 m
    5. TWI  = ln( a / max(tan beta, 1e-3) )
    6. streams = A > threshold   threshold calibrated against MERIT hnd ~ 0
    7. HAND = elev - elev(first stream cell reached by the D8 trace)

  FOUR THINGS THE BARE RECIPE NEEDS:

  (i)   HAND NEEDS D8 EVEN THOUGH TWI NEEDS MFD.  Under MFD flow splits across
        several downslope neighbours, so there is no single "the stream cell it
        drains to" to trace.  Two flow-direction products from one conditioned
        DEM.  pyflwdir and WhiteboxTools both do exactly this.

  (ii)  CONDITION ON A BUFFER, THEN CROP.  A window edge otherwise behaves as a
        wall: flow that should leave ponds against it, and depressions touching
        the edge fill to spurious levels.

  (iii) ASSERT THE ACCUMULATION UNIT.  Libraries return cell COUNTS or AREA.
        a = n*cellsize is the count version.  A factor of 30 inside a log is a
        silent constant offset that nothing downstream will catch.

  (iv)  SLOPE FLOOR 1e-3 (0.057 deg), and A FLOORED AT ONE CELL AREA.  TxSON mean
        slope is 2.5-4.1 deg, so near-flat cells are common and tan beta -> 0
        sends TWI to +inf.  REPORT THE PER-TILE FRACTION HITTING THE FLOOR — a
        large fraction means TWI is degenerate there, not merely clipped.


5.3  WINDOW SIZE — THE LOAD-BEARING CHOICE

  KEY REFINEMENT: the non-local error is LARGELY COMMON-MODE within a tile.  The
  model uses within-tile contrast:

        dTWI = d ln(a) - d ln(tan beta)

  Two cells on the same hillslope both have small local catchments, and neither
  receives the upstream inflow, so a truncated window gets their DIFFERENCE right
  while getting both absolute values wrong.  A tile-constant offset is removed by
  standardisation anyway.

  It breaks specifically at CHANNEL CELLS, which receive the outside inflow while
  adjacent hillslope cells do not.  Hence a checkable criterion:

      A TILE IS AT RISK IF AND ONLY IF IT CONTAINS A CHANNEL
      — read straight off MERIT `upa` inside the 2.24 km tile.

  BUT `a` STILL MATTERS WITHIN A TILE, so it cannot be left at MERIT's 90 m.
  Order of magnitude across one hillslope:

      a     ~30 m at the top  ->  ~500 m near the base   =>  d ln(a)      ~ 2.8
      tanb   0.02             ->   0.10                  =>  d ln(tan b)  ~ 1.6

  The `a` term carries AT LEAST AS MUCH within-tile contrast as the slope term.

  PROPOSED WINDOW: 10 km at 30 m = 333^2 cells ~ 0.44 MB/station, ~440 MB for all
  993.  Sized up per station where MERIT `upa` says the catchment is larger.
  Compute and storage were never the constraint.


5.4  MERIT'S ROLE — VALIDATOR AND CALIBRATOR, NOT PRODUCT

    upa INSIDE the tile       -> does this tile contain a channel?  (5.3)
    upa AT the station        -> how wide the window must be
    our accumulation vs MERIT -> PER-STATION BOUNDARY-CAPTURE PASS/FAIL,
                                 against ground truth
    hnd ~ 0 cells             -> calibrates our stream threshold instead of us
                                 picking one by hand
    —                         -> fallback product where no window is practical

  ~10 KB per station, and it turns every downstream choice from an assumption
  into a measurement.


5.5  THE LARGEST CORRECTNESS RISK: GLO-30 IS A DSM

  Copernicus GLO-30 INCLUDES CANOPY AND BUILDINGS.  A 20 m tree line dams a
  stream and conditioning then routes flow around a ridge that does not exist.
  This is precisely why MERIT Hydro exists as a separate product — it removed
  absolute bias, stripes, speckle AND tree height.

  Prefer FABDEM (GLO-30 with forest and buildings removed; CC-BY-NC, fine for
  research — CONFIRM THE ACCESS ROUTE, do not assume it).  Failing that, BREACH
  rather than fill (whitebox BreachDepressionsLeastCost, or richdem
  Priority-Flood): breaching carves through obstructions instead of drowning them
  in flat filled areas, and behaves markedly better under canopy.

  NONE of pyflwdir / richdem / whitebox / pysheds is installed in either conda
  env (verified).  pyflwdir (Deltares) is the best single fit — flow direction,
  accumulation and hand() in one numba-backed library, developed against MERIT so
  the conventions match.


5.6  THE THEORY, AND WHY IT DICTATES BLOCK 3

  TWI is TOPMODEL (Beven & Kirkby 1979).  Assuming the water table parallels the
  surface, steady state, and T = T0*exp(-f*z):

        R*a = T0 exp(-f z) tan beta
          z = (1/f) [ ln(T0/R) - ln(a/tan beta) ]
            = (1/f) [ ln(T0/R) - TWI ]

  DEPTH TO WATER TABLE IS LINEAR IN -TWI.  That is the entire theoretical
  content.  TWI is a WATER-TABLE-DEPTH index.

  HAND (Renno et al. 2008; Nobre et al. 2011) approximates the same quantity more
  directly — the stream is the local hydraulic datum — with no steady-state,
  recharge or transmissivity assumption.  It is also numerically more robust: a
  height difference, where TWI's `a` is very sensitive to the flow algorithm.

  NEITHER PREDICTS *SURFACE* MOISTURE except through water-table coupling.
  Capillary rise is ~0.1-0.3 m in sand, ~1-2 m in silt, 3-10 m in clay.  Once
  HAND exceeds a few metres the top 10 cm is hydraulically DECOUPLED, and is
  governed instead by infiltration, evaporative demand and the retention curve —
  a 1-D VERTICAL process, not TOPMODEL's lateral one.  TxSON has 46-88 m relief.

  THE EMPIRICAL RESULT THAT MATTERS MOST — Grayson et al. (1997), and the
  Tarrawarra work of Western, Grayson & Bloschl: terrain control of surface soil
  moisture is STATE-DEPENDENT.

      WET state -> lateral flow organises the pattern, terrain explains much of
                   the variance                            ("nonlocal control")
      DRY state -> soil and vegetation dominate, terrain explains ~nothing
                                                            ("local control")

  ==> THIS IS WHY BLOCK 3 IS THE RIGHT ARCHITECTURE.
      If the effect is state-dependent, feeding TWI as a static input that shifts
      a mean is the WRONG FUNCTIONAL FORM — it fits the average of two regimes
      and matches neither.  What is required is terrain INTERACTED WITH WETNESS
      STATE.  That is exactly what Block 3's cross-attention provides and exactly
      what a uniform FiLM scale/shift cannot express.

  TESTABLE PREDICTIONS.  Terrain should explain dSM better:
      in WET periods than dry        (sharpest; split by antecedent precipitation)
      at 30-50 cm than at 0-10 cm    (deeper is closer to saturation deficit)
      where HAND is SMALL            (metres, not tens of metres)
      in humid low-relief settings   (rather than semi-arid)
  If dSM is flat across all four splits the mechanism is absent, and no DEM
  resolution rescues it.

  THE COMPETING MECHANISM, STATED FAIRLY.  If lateral flow does not reach 0-10 cm,
  heterogeneity still comes from somewhere — Grayson's "local controls": texture,
  vegetation, aspect-driven insolation.  §30.3a measured these varying within our
  tiles (texture 0.26-0.29, socd/soc 0.50-0.54, northness 0.51), and CR200-18's
  full-tile range is 11 wt% clay / 22.75 wt% sand.  TWI and HAND address ONLY ONE
  of the two candidate mechanisms.

9.1  THE RULE THAT MAKES A 32 m OUTPUT DEFENSIBLE

  The criterion was never resolution.  It is WHERE SUB-TOKEN DETAIL COMES FROM:

                        │ 14x14 out │ unpatchify   │ gather + measured rasters
    ────────────────────┼───────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────────────
    output grid         │   160 m   │     32 m     │         32 m
    sub-token detail    │     —     │ token embed  │    MEASURED PIXELS
    defensible under    │    yes    │      NO      │         YES
    1 label per tile    │           │              │

  Unpatchify is rejected because the network would INVENT sub-token structure
  with nothing to check it — §17.7's objection to the DiVAE, and what §23 caught
  the decoder actually doing.  A nearest gather of the 160 m token concatenated
  with MEASURED 32 m channels invents nothing: every variation traces to a
  measurement.

9.2  NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

  * NEAREST GATHER, NEVER BILINEAR.
  * BLOCKINESS IS A DIAGNOSTIC, NOT A DEFECT.  If the fine channels are inert,
    the output shows 5x5 blocks.  THAT IS THE SIGNAL that Block 4 is contributing
    nothing.  Bilinear would hide exactly that failure behind a smooth ramp —
    which is how the current map looks plausible while being anti-correlated with
    the landscape.  DO NOT SMOOTH IT AWAY.  REPORT IT.
  * NO WITHIN-BLOCK COORDINATE FEATURE (i%5, j%5).  It would let the model paint
    arbitrary sub-token patterns from position alone.
  * EVERY FINE CHANNEL MUST BE MEASURED.  No learned upsampling anywhere.

9.3  UNetDecoder IS DELETED FROM THE FORWARD PATH

  model.py:188 — four upsampling stages at 512/256/128/64 plus three FiLM'd skips
  (L3/L6/L9), all modulated by the spatially-constant `context`.  Kept runnable
  behind --head unet for the regression gate and rollback.

  §28.2's figure is ~3.2 M activations/sample/depth against ~200 K here, and the
  history payload halves, so TRAINING GETS CHEAPER, not dearer.

PART 10 — THE LST AUXILIARY
================================================================================

10.1  WHERE IT ATTACHES

  A side branch off S — NOT off h_k.  Hanging it off h_k would require running
  Block 3 at all 196 locations every training step, destroying the entire reason
  training is cheap.  S is already computed for every location, so the branch
  costs one linear layer.

      head_LST = Linear(768 -> 1)  applied to each of the 196 vectors in S
               -> (B,196) -> reshape (B,14,14)

10.2  THE TARGET

  Landsat surface temperature, averaged into each 160 m token to give a 14x14
  map, then MINUS THE TILE MEAN FOR THAT DATE.

  Subtracting the tile mean is not a detail.  Absolute LST is dominated by
  season, time of day and air temperature — all of which the model can already
  read off ERA5, so predicting it teaches nothing about space.  The deviation
  from the tile mean is the ONLY part that carries spatial information.  It also
  removes precisely the thing that manufactured §29's spurious +0.167.

  Mask the tokens with no retrieval.

10.3  THE LOSS

      L = L_SM  +  lambda * L_LST

  lambda balanced by GRADIENT MAGNITUDE, not by counting pixels — ~196 LST values
  against 1 SM value means a naive sum drowns soil moisture completely.  Decay
  lambda over training: early for representation learning, late to avoid
  contaminating the soil moisture map.

10.4  WHY IT IS SMALLER THAN IT LOOKS

  In the §30 design, with the U-Net, a dense target was LOAD-BEARING: it was the
  only thing preventing the decoder from inventing sub-token structure, which is
  what §16.4 demonstrated.

  But Block 4 is not a decoder.  It is a small function of measured channels.  It
  has nothing to paint with.  So the problem LST was meant to solve has largely
  dissolved on its own, and the auxiliary's job changes from CONSTRAINING THE
  DECODER to GIVING BLOCK 1 DENSE GRADIENT.  Real, but a nice-to-have rather than
  structural.

10.5  FOUR HONEST CAVEATS

  (i)   "5776 px vs 1" IS THE GRID, NOT THE INFORMATION.  Landsat TIRS is 100 m;
        the ST product ships at 30 m but is resampled.  Real independent values
        are ~22x22 ~ 500 per tile.  Still ~500x better than 1 — but quote the
        honest number.

  (ii)  THE PATTERN IS STATIC, so it disciplines the WRONG BRANCH.  §29 measured
        +0.967 month-to-month spatial correlation.  Predicting LST therefore
        reduces to predicting a time-invariant map, which the model can learn
        from terrain/LULC/soil without engaging the temporal pathway at all.  It
        constrains the STATIC representation.  §31's thesis is that the RESPONSE
        FUNCTION is what is spatially constant.  Useful, but not the headline.

  (iii) THE MISSINGNESS IS BIASED.  18.2% of the tile has no ST retrieval, and
        §29 found that region CONTAINS THE WETTEST STATION.  That is structured
        missingness correlated with the target — it would teach a map with a hole
        exactly where the wet places are.

  (iv)  IT MAY BE MEMORISED RATHER THAN LEARNED.  If the target is a fixed map
        per station, and the model can identify the station — §24.12 found
        identity dominates the representation at depth, and §27b's UMAP organised
        cleanly by Koppen class — then it can memorise 993 maps instead of
        learning anything about landscape structure.  This is likely, not
        hypothetical.  TEST IT IN THE FIRST SMOKE RUN by comparing LST-head skill
        on validation stations against training stations.  A large gap means
        memorisation, and the auxiliary should be dropped or switched to per-date
        targets, which vary and are harder to memorise.

10.6  THE CHEAP HALF, WORTH DOING REGARDLESS

  Because the pattern is static, the time-averaged anomaly map is a perfectly
  good INPUT channel — lst_hi in 4.3.  A measured ~100 m landscape descriptor,
  one extra column in Block 4.  No loss balancing, no lambda, no contamination
  risk.  It does nothing for supervision density, so it is not a substitute for
  the auxiliary head, but it is nearly free.

11.2  ONE TRAINING STEP

  The dataloader worker opens the station's zarr, pulls the anchor tokens,
  indexes column k out of the S2 and S1 token grids, takes the terrain and fine
  stacks, ERA5, SIF, TWSA, soil and the three depth labels, computes pixel_idx
  and token_idx, and applies the translation crop.  The prefetcher moves the
  batch to GPU while the previous step is still computing.

  Forward: Block 1 over the whole sequence -> S, g.  Block 2 -> W.  Block 3 at
  ONE location.  Block 4 at ONE cell.  Huber against the label.  If the auxiliary
  is on, the LST head runs over all 196 vectors of S and adds its term.

  Backward, step.  Four H100s, DDP, bf16 autocast.

11.3  WHAT TO WATCH EVERY EPOCH, NOT AT THE END

  * PEAK MEMORY AND DATA-LOADING TIME against the 540 GB baseline.  What crosses

    the decoder is gone — but "should" is not "did".

  * STANDARD DEVIATION OF PREDICTIONS ACROSS LOCATIONS.  If this sits near zero,

PART 15 — RISKS, STATED BEFORE THE GPU IS SPENT
================================================================================

THE PHYSICS MAY NOT REACH 0-10 cm.  The largest risk, and INDEPENDENT OF DEM
RESOLUTION.  TWI and HAND are saturation-excess concepts (5.6).  Our labels are
the top 10 cm, governed in most settings by rainfall and evaporation rather than
lateral redistribution.  Same shape as §29: a mechanism real in the literature,
plausible here, possibly absent from THIS signal at THIS depth.  §29.13 tested
evaporative cooling and measured -0.077.  This is why 14.3 runs on 90 m data IN
PARALLEL with the 30 m build, not after it.

§27b's CEILING MAY BE REAL.  Own-token within-network skill measured 0-4.2%.  If
a location's token carries that little, no architecture manufactures information.

BUT A TERRAIN FAILURE DOES NOT KILL THE PROJECT.  See PART 13, row 2.

THE §29 FALLBACK IS WEAKER THAN §30.6 ORIGINALLY CLAIMED.  §29.13 ran Phase A:
daytime Landsat LST does NOT track within-tile SM (within-station fixed effects
-0.077; the +0.167 pooled figure is a Simpson's-paradox artefact of station
identity).  It survives only on §29.9's grounds — LST as a DENSE AUXILIARY
TARGET, i.e. supervision density rather than correlation.  Phase B (ECOSTRESS
night LST and diurnal range) is untouched and is the only arm that could still
recover a direct LST<->SM link.

1. SCIENTIFIC DESCRIPTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Soil physical and chemical properties exert first-order control on soil
moisture dynamics through their regulation of hydraulic conductivity, water
retention, and rooting-depth storage capacity. To provide the multimodal
soil moisture model with spatially explicit, time-invariant soil context, we
extract per-station soil property patches from the OpenLandMap-soildb dataset
(Hengl et al., 2026), a global 30 m product derived from spatiotemporal machine
learning trained on over 363,000 harmonised legacy soil profile observations.

Seven soil properties are extracted at three standard depth intervals
(0–30 cm, 30–60 cm, 60–100 cm), yielding 21 channels per station patch.
The depth intervals correspond directly to the three soil moisture prediction
targets of our model (0–10/10–30 cm surface and root zone; 30–100 cm deep
root zone), allowing the soil encoder CNN to learn depth-specific relationships
between soil texture, organic carbon, bulk density, pH, and hydraulic
behaviour at each prediction depth. The 2020–2022 temporal composite is used
for all variables; while SOC and pH exhibit decadal trends, the 2020–2022
composite is the most accurate (largest training sample) and temporally
appropriate for the majority of station records (2014–2025).

Patches are centred on each station's measurement location and span a
physically consistent 2.22 km × 2.22 km footprint (74 × 74 pixels at 30 m)
across all latitudes. Geographic consistency is achieved by computing the
bounding box in UTM projected coordinates (±1,110 m from the station centre)
before transforming to WGS84 for the COG range request. This avoids the
east-west pixel shrinkage that would occur with a fixed degree-based bounding
box at high latitudes (e.g., at 69°N the east-west extent would be reduced by
~65% relative to the equator without UTM correction).

Data access uses HTTP range requests on Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs (COGs) served
from the OpenGeoHub S3 object store (s3.opengeohub.org). Because COGs index
their internal tile structure via HTTP range headers, only the bytes covering
the 74 × 74 px station window are transferred — typically 10–50 kB per band
per station — rather than the full 13–17 GB global files.

Spatial footprint
-----------------
  Target:     2.22 km × 2.22 km (= 74 × 30 m)
  Pixels:     74 × 74 (always, via bilinear resampling)
  BBox method:  UTM projection to ensure physical consistency:
                1. Determine UTM zone from station longitude
                   EPSG = 32600 + zone  (N hemisphere)
                   EPSG = 32700 + zone  (S hemisphere)
                2. Transform station lat/lon → UTM (cx, cy)
                3. half_m = 74/2 × 30 m = 1,110 m
                4. UTM bbox = (cx−1110, cy−1110, cx+1110, cy+1110)
                5. Transform 4 corners back to WGS84
                6. Use WGS84 bbox for COG window extraction
  Physical size at different latitudes (verified):
    Equator   (0°N):  2.218 km × 2.233 km
    UK        (51°N): 2.246 km × 2.192 km
    Finland   (68°N): 2.214 km × 2.217 km
    Tibet     (32°N): 2.176 km × 2.269 km
  Note: small deviations (<2%) from target due to UTM distortion at bbox corners.

Data transfer
-------------
  No full download: COG HTTP range requests transfer only the indexed tile(s)
  covering the station window — typically 10–50 kB per band per station.
  Total data transferred for 1,048 stations × 21 bands ≈ 200–500 MB.
  Compare with full file size: ~14 GB per variable × 21 = ~290 GB if downloaded.

Output
------
  Path:    {SATELLITE_DIR}/{network}_{station_id}/soil/soil_patch.tif
  Format:  Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFF, float32, deflate compressed
  Shape:   (21, 74, 74)
  CRS:     WGS84 (EPSG:4326), copied from source COG
  Tags:    per-band name, units, depth

Parallelism
-----------
  N_WORKERS = 8 concurrent threads (each opens rasterio handles independently)
  Runtime estimate: 1,048 stations × 21 COG reads × ~0.5 s/read / 8 workers
                    ≈ ~30–45 minutes on Snellius

Design decisions
----------------
  1. All 3 depth intervals: provides per-depth soil context matching the
     model's 3 SM depth bins (0-10, 10-30, 30-100 cm). The CNN learns
     depth-specific soil-hydraulic relationships without manual aggregation.

  2. 2020-2022 composite for all variables: clay/sand/silt only have one
     composite available; using the same period for all variables ensures
     temporal consistency across channels. Soil texture is quasi-static.

  3. UTM bbox vs degree bbox: a fixed-degree bbox would shrink east-west
     extent by cos(lat), giving a 0.73 km × 2.06 km patch at 69°N (59 of
     our 1,048 stations are above 60°N). UTM projection corrects this.

  4. COG range reads vs full download: the global files are 13–17 GB each.
     COG internal tiling (typically 256×256 px tiles) means only 1–4 HTTP
     range requests per station window per file, avoiding bulk transfer.
